# Pensions Law Gold Standard Output Shapes

Internal QA only. Do not copy this wording into user answers.

## Shape 1: NRA, Equalisation, Amendment And Calculations Problem

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: short answer and route-map. State the likely answer on each requested calculation/legal issue.
- Part II: assumptions and missing facts. Identify scheme registration, exact amendment wording, member consent, actuarial certificate, relevant dates, DB/DC status and whether the member is active/deferred/pensioner.
- Part III: scheme and statutory framework. Start with the deed/rules and amendment power, then add Barber, the sex equality rule, Pensions Act 1995 section 67 where applicable, Equality Act 2010 exceptions and any current-law gender-recognition issue if raised.
- Part IV: calculations. Show service periods, NRA for each tranche, formula, early-retirement adjustment, commutation factor, rounding and total. Keep law and arithmetic connected.
- Part V: validity of amendment. Analyse power, scope, proper purpose, Imperial/Braganza type constraints, due process, subsisting-rights protection and any actuarial certificate/consent route.
- Part VI: differentiated commutation/gender issue. Separate equality rule, actuarial-factor exception, unsettled arguments and current-law caution.
- Part VII: final answer. Give a practical result, amount/range if calculable, residual assumptions and next evidence needed.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Defines NRA, DB, DC and any technical acronyms on first use.
- Uses the scheme wording and statutory route before policy discussion.
- Shows visible workings, not only final figures.
- Treats Barber timing and domestic equalisation/amendment mechanics separately.
- Flags missing registration, consent and actuarial certificate facts instead of assuming them.
- Uses calibrated language where the law is unsettled.

### Common Fail Signals
- Assuming section 67 automatically changes all private scheme rules without testing the amendment route.
- Treating every adverse amendment as invalid without checking timing, power and statutory protection.
- Omitting arithmetic or hiding calculations in prose.
- Conflating biological sex, legal gender and actuarial-factor exceptions without current-law caution.
- Loose pinpoints, undefined acronyms or unexplained assumptions.

## Shape 2: Non-Financial Investment / ESG / Member Preference Problem

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: conclusion. State whether trustees should accept or reject each proposed investment/divestment request.
- Part II: assumptions. Identify scheme purpose, private/public scheme status, DB/DC structure, default fund, employer covenant, Statement of Investment Principles, adviser input and survey quality.
- Part III: governing framework. Start with scheme purpose: providing retirement benefits. Then apply trustee investment duty, best financial interests, statutory investment regulations and the two-stage non-financial factor test.
- Part IV: proposal-by-proposal analysis. For each request: characterise whether it is financially material ESG or non-financial preference; analyse member consensus; analyse significant financial detriment; analyse employer covenant and impartiality.
- Part V: process and evidence. Address investment advice, survey scope, whether members were informed of trade-offs, consultation with employer, SIP amendment and record-keeping.
- Part VI: final advice. Accept, reject or defer pending evidence, with practical steps.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Starts non-financial investment answers with the scheme's retirement-benefit purpose.
- Separates financially material risk from non-financial ethical preference.
- Tests member support across the relevant beneficiary body, not only active members or a vocal group.
- Evaluates financial detriment at asset, member, employer-covenant and scheme-funding level.
- Explains trustee impartiality where members are divided.
- States whether the request should be rejected, implemented, or investigated further.

### Common Fail Signals
- Treating ethical agreement from some members as enough.
- Ignoring pensioners, deferred members, DC default members or survivor beneficiaries.
- Treating public-sector pension authority constraints as automatically applicable to private occupational trusts.
- Ignoring employer covenant where DB funding risk is affected.
- Praising ESG in general terms without applying the legal test.
