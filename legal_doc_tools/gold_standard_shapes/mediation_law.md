# International Commercial Mediation Gold Standard Output Shapes

Internal QA only. Do not copy this wording into user answers.

## Shape 1: Singapore Convention / Critical Mass / Reform Essay

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: thesis-led introduction. Define the Singapore Convention on Mediation, the benchmark used for success, and the specific defect or reform target.
- Part II: benchmark. Explain why the New York Convention is the comparator only by function: portable enforcement, narrow refusal grounds, commercial predictability and network effects.
- Part III: mediation's enforcement gap. Explain why mediated settlement agreements were weaker than arbitral awards before direct enforcement and why contract-only enforcement is commercially unattractive.
- Part IV: ratification and adoption problem. Analyse critical mass, state incentives, private-sector demand, lawyer/in-house counsel risk aversion and circular adoption problems.
- Part V: doctrinal defect. Use Article 5 refusal grounds, especially mediator misconduct or serious breach, to show how uncertainty enters the enforcement stage.
- Part VI: confidentiality and evidence tension. Explain how proving misconduct or causation may require opening the mediation process and generate satellite litigation.
- Part VII: reform. Propose a precise legal mechanism: protocol, model implementing legislation, safe-harbour standards, red-list misconduct, objective materiality or clearer burden.
- Part VIII: conclusion. State whether the reform would materially improve mediation's commercial status without turning mediation into arbitration.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Defines Singapore Convention, New York Convention and critical mass before evaluation.
- Distinguishes treaty signature, ratification/accession, entry into force, domestic implementation and party reliance.
- Treats states as ratifying/implementing and commercial parties as using/drafting around the regime.
- Explains the enforceability gap before criticising Convention limits.
- Identifies the exact Article 5-style refusal concern, seriousness threshold, causation/materiality and evidence burden.
- Links reform to commercial predictability, not just abstract fairness.

### Common Fail Signals
- Saying mediation will succeed because ADR is good.
- Treating soft-law notes, protocols or institutional guidance as binding law without an incorporation mechanism.
- Treating the Singapore Convention as identical to arbitration or the New York Convention.
- Ignoring confidentiality when discussing mediator misconduct evidence.
- Proposing reform without explaining how it would bind courts, states, mediators or parties.

## Shape 2: Mediation Clause / Confidentiality / Settlement Enforcement Problem

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: short advice. Identify the immediate procedural route and practical objective.
- Part II: classify the object. Separate agreement to mediate, mediation process, mediator conduct and mediated settlement agreement.
- Part III: clause enforceability. Decide whether the clause is an unenforceable agreement to negotiate, a sufficiently certain agreement to mediate, or a binding multi-tier process.
- Part IV: confidentiality and privilege. Separate contractual confidentiality, equitable confidence, without-prejudice privilege, mediator compellability, waiver and exceptions.
- Part V: settlement enforcement. Choose the route: contract claim, Tomlin/consent order, arbitral consent award, Singapore Convention enforcement or domestic implementation route.
- Part VI: refusal/defence analysis. If cross-border enforcement is raised, apply Convention scope, international/commercial status, exclusions and Article 5 refusal grounds.
- Part VII: practical next steps. Draft/reserve rights, preserve confidentiality, prepare evidence, consider costs sanctions and identify settlement amendment/enforcement strategy.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Keeps mediation distinct from litigation and arbitration.
- Does not treat settlement as automatically enforceable.
- Names the legal source of confidentiality and each exception relied on.
- Analyses mediator conduct separately from settlement validity.
- Addresses bad-faith participation, power imbalance or pressure where facts raise it.
- Ends with a practical route, not only legal commentary.

### Common Fail Signals
- Calling an agreement to negotiate, agreement to mediate and settlement agreement the same thing.
- Saying mediation is confidential without source or exception.
- Importing arbitration doctrines without explaining the comparison.
- Ignoring costs consequences of refusal or silence where ADR was invited.
- Omitting the forum/enforcement route in a cross-border settlement problem.
