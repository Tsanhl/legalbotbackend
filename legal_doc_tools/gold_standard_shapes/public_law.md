# Public Law Gold Standard Output Shapes

Internal QA only. Do not copy this wording into user answers.

## Shape 1: Legitimate Expectation / Improper Purpose Problem

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: short advice. Identify claimant, decision, decision-maker, power source and desired remedy.
- Part II: justiciability, standing and time.
- Part III: legitimate expectation. Distinguish procedural/substantive expectation; apply clear representation or settled practice, qualification, reliance/fairness, frustration and overriding public interest.
- Part IV: illegality grounds. Analyse improper purpose, relevant/irrelevant considerations, fettering, reasons and consultation separately where facts raise them.
- Part V: intensity. Explain why review is legality, proportionality or anxious scrutiny depending on context.
- Part VI: remedy. Quashing, declaration, mandatory/prohibiting order, interim relief, damages only if separate route; explain discretion and practical consequence.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Starts with power source and remedy sought.
- Does not write generic "unfair/irrational".
- Separates legitimate expectation from ordinary procedural fairness.
- Explains why the remedy is discretionary.

### Common Fail Signals
- Treating political embarrassment or poor administration as enough without a legal ground.
- Using proportionality in a non-rights context without explanation.
- Omitting standing/time or remedy discretion.

## Shape 2: HRA / Proportionality Problem Or Essay

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: claimant, public authority and engaged right.
- Part II: interference and legality.
- Part III: legitimate aim.
- Part IV: necessity/proportionality, including less intrusive alternatives and fair balance.
- Part V: remedy and institutional competence.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Keeps engagement, interference, legality, aim and proportionality separate.
- Does not substitute policy preference for legal proportionality.
