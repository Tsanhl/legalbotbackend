# Competition Law Gold Standard Output Shapes

Internal QA only. Do not copy this wording into user answers.

## Shape 1: Article 102 Digital Platform / Self-Preferencing Problem

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: short enforcement-risk view.
- Part II: undertaking, jurisdiction and market definition. Define only as far as needed for market power and theory of harm.
- Part III: dominance. Use shares, barriers, network effects, data advantage, switching costs, ecosystem lock-in and countervailing buyer power.
- Part IV: abuse theory. Choose the strongest theory: self-preferencing, tying, refusal/access, margin squeeze, discriminatory terms or exploitative terms.
- Part V: effects evidence. Explain foreclosure mechanism, counterfactual, as-efficient competitor evidence where relevant, traffic/data diversion and consumer/innovation harm.
- Part VI: objective justification. Identify aim, evidence, necessity, proportionality, less restrictive alternatives and efficiencies.
- Part VII: enforcement and remedy. Commitments, cease-and-desist, access/interoperability remedy, fine exposure, damages and compliance steps.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Separates dominance from abuse.
- Uses a concrete mechanism of foreclosure or exploitation.
- Does not become "big tech bad" policy.
- Treats privacy/security as possible justification but demands evidence and proportionality.
- Ends with enforcement risk and remedy.

### Common Fail Signals
- Long market-definition essay that never connects to the abuse.
- Labelling self-preferencing without explaining ranking/default/data/access mechanism.
- Objective justification as one sentence.
- Ignoring counterfactual and effects evidence.
