# Evidence Law Gold Standard Output Shapes

Internal QA only. Do not copy this wording into user answers.

## Shape 1: Multi-Item Criminal Admissibility Problem

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: trial posture and item list. Identify prosecution or defence use for each item.
- Part II: item-by-item classification. Hearsay, bad character, confession, identification, expert evidence, previous statement or privilege.
- Part III: gateway. Apply the exact statutory or common-law route for each item.
- Part IV: exclusion/fairness. Use PACE, CJA fairness controls and Article 6 only after the domestic route.
- Part V: weight and safeguards. State warning, direction, editing, limited use or withdrawal from jury.
- Part VI: final rulings table: admitted, excluded, admitted with limits, or needs further voir dire/evidence.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Classifies evidence before applying a rule.
- Separates admissibility from weight.
- States the judge's likely ruling.
- Uses Article 6 as overall fairness context, not as a shortcut.
- Gives safeguards where risky evidence is admitted.

### Common Fail Signals
- "Relevant, therefore admissible".
- Merging hearsay, bad character and previous statements.
- Ignoring whether evidence is prosecution or defence evidence.
- Omitting Turnbull, PACE section 76/78, CJA 2003 gateway or expert reliability when raised.

## Shape 2: Fair Trial / Reliability Essay

### Ideal Output Structure
- Part I: thesis on truth-finding, reliability and fair-trial protection.
- Part II: statutory architecture and judicial discretion.
- Part III: reliability controls, using two or three focused examples.
- Part IV: rights/fairness critique, including Article 6.
- Part V: reform or defence of current law.

### Must-Have Quality Checks
- Uses examples in depth rather than surveying every evidence rule.
- Connects policy to statutory gateway and trial consequence.
